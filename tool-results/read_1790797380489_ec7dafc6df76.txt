     1→# RequirementPilot
     2→## Agentic, Evidence-Grounded Requirements Engineering for Real Stakeholder Conversations
     3→
     4→> **Project reference document — research + product + implementation blueprint**
     5→>
     6→> **Research snapshot:** 30 September 2026
     7→>
     8→> **Status:** Graduation / Internship Project Reference
     9→>
    10→> **Working title:** RequirementPilot
    11→>
    12→> **Core idea:** Build an AI requirements engineer that does not merely generate requirements from a prompt. It conducts a structured stakeholder interview, identifies missing/ambiguous/conflicting information, preserves evidence and decisions, turns validated information into a living requirements model, and propagates approved changes through downstream artifacts.
    13→
    14→---
    15→
    16→## 0. Important positioning decision
    17→
    18→The original idea of a generic **"AI Requirements Engineer"** is no longer sufficiently novel by itself. Research and commercial systems already cover requirement extraction, user-story generation, acceptance-criteria generation, conversational elicitation, latent requirement discovery, conflict detection, change-impact analysis, requirements-to-code traceability, and multi-agent requirements/architecture workflows.
    19→
    20→Therefore, this project must **not** be presented as:
    21→
    22→> "An LLM that converts stakeholder text into requirements."
    23→
    24→The project should instead be positioned as:
    25→
    26→> **"A human-in-the-loop, evidence-grounded, change-aware requirements engineering system that actively interviews stakeholders, manages uncertainty and conflicts, and maintains a traceable requirements lifecycle."**
    27→
    28→The strongest differentiating direction is not one isolated feature. It is the **integration of several RE controls into one living, auditable system**, plus a focused research contribution around **question selection, evidence/provenance, uncertainty, and Arabic/Egyptian-Arabic mixed-language requirements elicitation**.
    29→
    30→**Novelty warning:** this document deliberately does **not** claim that every proposed feature is globally unprecedented. The literature is changing extremely quickly. The novelty statements below mean that, in the sources searched for this project, an **exact combination or exact implementation was not found**. Before submitting a paper or claiming "first," perform a final systematic search and citation check.

    34→# 1. Executive Summary
    35→
    36→## 1.1 The problem
    37→
    38→Real stakeholders rarely provide clean software requirements.
    39→
    40→A stakeholder may say:
    41→
    42→> "عايز العميل يقدر يلغي الأوردر، بس بعد ما السواق يتحرك الموضوع يختلف، ولو المشكلة من عندنا الفلوس لازم ترجعله. وكمان المدير يشوف كل حاجة."
    43→
    44→This single statement contains multiple requirement candidates, exceptions, an undefined policy boundary, an actor distinction, and an implied business rule.
    45→
    46→A conventional LLM can immediately generate polished user stories. That is dangerous because the polished language can hide missing information rather than solve it.
    47→
    48→The real requirements-engineering problem is:
    49→
    50→1. identify what was actually stated;
    51→2. distinguish explicit needs from inferred possibilities;
    52→3. detect missing information;
    53→4. detect ambiguous language;
    54→5. detect conflicts between requirements or stakeholders;
    55→6. ask the next question that provides the most useful clarification;
    56→7. preserve source evidence and rationale;
    57→8. transform validated answers into structured requirements;
    58→9. connect requirements to acceptance criteria, tests, design artifacts and implementation tasks;
    59→10. detect downstream impact when a requirement changes;
    60→11. maintain a history of what changed, why, who approved it, and what remains unresolved.

    64→# 2. What existing research already covers
    65→
    66→The project was benchmarked against recent research through September 2026.
    67→
    68→## 2.1 RECOVER — requirements from stakeholder conversations
    69→
    70→**RECOVER (Requirements EliCitation frOm conVERsations)** analyzes stakeholder conversations and automatically extracts system requirements. It evaluates both turn-level identification and conversation-level synthesis, including correctness, completeness and actionability. The work appeared in *IEEE Transactions on Software Engineering* and was also presented at ICSE 2026.
    71→
    72→Key implication for this project:
    73→
    74→> "I analyze stakeholder conversations and generate requirements" is already known.
    75→
    76→Source:
    77→- IEEE / institutional record: https://doi.org/10.1109/TSE.2025.3572056
    78→- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19552
    79→- ICSE 2026: https://conf.researchr.org/details/icse-2026/icse-2026-journal-first-papers/71/RECOVER-Toward-Requirements-Generation-from-Stakeholders-Conversations
    80→
    81→---
    82→
    83→## 2.2 LENS — latent requirements from stakeholder interviews
    84→
    85→A June 2026 preprint, **LENS (LLM-Enabled Needs Discovery from Stakeholder Interviews)**, goes beyond explicit extraction. It analyzes interview transcripts and organizational context to infer **latent requirements** that stakeholders did not directly articulate. Each inferred requirement is accompanied by rationale and transcript traceability. The preliminary industrial evaluation used twelve cybersecurity-operations interviews and reported an average explicit-requirement F1 of 84.4%; 75% of latent requirements were rated by domain experts as having useful automation/time-saving potential.
    86→
    87→Key implication:
    88→
    89→> "We will discover hidden/implicit requirements from conversations" is no longer safe as the only novelty.
    90→
    91→Source:
    92→- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25867
    93→
    94→Important detail: LENS is especially relevant because it already uses an **explicit vs. latent requirement distinction**, organizational context, rationale generation, and source traceability.
    95→

    97→
    98→## 2.3 OntoAgent — agentic requirements interview
    99→
   100→A May 2026 paper, **From Chat to Interview: Agentic Requirements Elicitation with an Experience Ontology**, introduces OntoAgent. It argues that free-form LLM chat can miss implicit requirements and ask redundant questions. OntoAgent first induces an **experience ontology** from domain requirements and then uses operations called `ParseUser`, `ScoreOnto`, `ReRankOnto`, and `GatePrune` to select requirement concerns and generate interview questions.
   101→
   102→The paper evaluates the approach on website-application requirements and reports a 33% improvement in IRE and 21% improvement in TKQR relative to the comparison baselines.
   103→
   104→Key implication:
   105→
   106→> "Our novelty is an agent that interviews the stakeholder instead of just chatting" is already being actively researched.
   107→
   108→Source:
   109→- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05828
   110→
   111→Related code/evaluation ecosystem:
   112→- ReqElicitBench / ReqElicitGym: https://github.com/jdm4pku/ReqElicitBench
   113→
   114→---
   115→
   116→## 2.4 ReqElicitGym — evaluation of interview competence
   117→
   118→The ReqElicitGym repository provides an interactive evaluation environment for conversational requirements elicitation. It contains 101 website requirements-elicitation scenarios across 10 application types, an LLM-based simulated stakeholder, a judge, conversation logs, and quantitative metrics.
   119→
   120→Key implication:
   121→
   122→> The project should not invent an evaluation strategy from scratch; it should build on established benchmark ideas while adding a new test dimension such as Arabic/Egyptian Arabic, evidence quality, or cross-artifact impact.
   123→
   124→Source:
   125→- GitHub: https://github.com/jdm4pku/ReqElicitBench

   129→## 2.5 ProReFiCIA — requirements change-impact analysis
   130→
   131→ProReFiCIA is an LLM-driven change-impact analysis approach published in 2026. It takes a natural-language change rationale and identifies the affected requirements. The final version reports 85.7% recall on an unseen industrial dataset while requiring review of only about 3.0% of requirements; adding domain knowledge through RAG raised recall to 95.8% while increasing the review share to about 3.4%.
   132→
   133→Its workflow includes prompt optimization, model/prompt selection, refinement, filtering, and domain-knowledge augmentation.
   134→
   135→Key implication:
   136→
   137→> "Our system tells which requirements are affected when one requirement changes" is also already a standalone research contribution.
   138→
   139→Source:
   140→- arXiv HTML: https://arxiv.org/html/2511.00262v6
   141→- arXiv record: https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.00262

   145→## 2.6 Arabic requirements conflict detection — AraREQ
   146→
   147→AraREQ, published at LREC 2026, provides a large Arabic dataset for requirements conflict detection and resolution.    148→
   149→Key implication:

   154→- ACL Anthology: https://aclanthology.org/2026.lrec-1.553/

   201→## 2.9 Multi-agent architecture from requirements is already active
   202→
   203→MAAD (Multi-Agent Architecture Design), introduced in 2026, uses four agents — Analyst, Modeler, Designer and Evaluator — plus RAG and hierarchical memory to transform requirements into architecture blueprints. It evaluates architecture completeness, modularity, traceability and quality attributes.
   204→
   205→Sources:
   206→- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01385
   207→- replication package: https://github.com/RuiyinL/MAAD
   208→
   209→Therefore, the project should not claim novelty merely by adding multiple agents.

   213→## 2.10 Multi-agent stakeholder negotiation is also emerging
   214→
   215→MedNegotiator, published in 2026, applies persona-driven generative AI agents, utility functions, Nash bargaining and safety guards to requirements negotiation in healthcare.
   216→
   217→Source:
   218→- https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2590005626001062
   219→
   220→This means that "multiple agents negotiate requirements" is not untouched territory. A differentiated implementation would need a different research question or context.

   230→> **RequirementPilot is an evidence-first, change-aware, human-in-the-loop requirements operating system for ambiguous stakeholder conversations.**

   236→# 4. Proposed novelty contributions
   237→
   238→The following are **candidate novelty contributions**, ordered from the most central to optional.
   239→
   240→## N1 — Evidence-first Requirement Objects
   241→
   242→Every requirement in the system must contain provenance and evidence metadata.
   243→
   244→A requirement should never exist as only:
   245→
   246→```text
   247→REQ-017: The customer can cancel an order before dispatch.
   248→```
   249→
   250→Instead, it should be modeled as:
   251→
   252→```json
   253→{
   254→  "id": "REQ-017",
   255→  "statement": "The customer can cancel an order before dispatch.",
   256→  "type": "functional",
   257→  "status": "proposed",
   258→  "source_spans": ["conversation_12", "turn_38"],
   259→  "evidence_level": "explicit",
   260→  "evidence_text": "...",
   261→  "speaker": "customer",
   262→  "stakeholder_role": "customer",
   263→  "rationale": "...",
   264→  "assumptions": [],
   265→  "ambiguities": [],
   266→  "confidence": 0.96,
   267→  "validated_by": null,
   268→  "approval_status": "pending",
   269→  "created_by": "elicitation_agent",
   270→  "created_at": "...",
   271→  "version": 1
   272→}
   273→```
   274→
   275→### Why this matters
   276→
   277→It creates a distinction between:
   278→
   279→- what the stakeholder explicitly said;
   280→- what the system inferred;
   281→- what the analyst proposed;
   282→- what a stakeholder validated;
   283→- what remains uncertain.
   284→
   285→The system must never silently convert an inference into an official requirement.
   286→
   287→### Candidate research question
   288→
   289→> Does explicit evidence classification and provenance reduce hallucinated or unsupported requirements compared with conventional LLM-only requirements generation?
   290→

   292→
   293→# 5. N2 — Adaptive Question Selection using uncertainty + information gain + impact
   294→
   295→OntoAgent already uses ontology-guided question selection. The project can go in a different direction by treating the interview as an **active uncertainty-reduction problem**.
   296→
   297→At each turn, the system maintains a set of unresolved requirement uncertainties:
   298→
   299→```text
   300→U1: Who can cancel an order?
   301→U2: When can cancellation occur?
   302→U3: Is refund automatic?
   303→U4: What exceptions exist?
   304→U5: What happens if payment already settled?
   305→```
   306→
   307→Each candidate question gets a utility score:
   308→
   309→```text
   310→Question Utility =
   311→    Information Gain
   312→  × Requirement Impact
   313→  × Ambiguity Reduction
   314→  × Downstream Risk
   315→  - User Effort
   316→  - Redundancy
   317→```
   318→
   319→The question is not simply "What sounds reasonable?"
   320→
   321→It is:
   322→
   323→> "Which unanswered question will remove the most uncertainty from the requirements model while causing the least stakeholder burden?"
   324→
   325→Expected information gain is an established concept for asking informative questions in LLM systems, so the novelty here should be the **requirements-engineering objective function and downstream impact weighting**, not the information-gain concept itself.
   326→
   327→Relevant general research:
   328→- https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.291/
   329→
   330→Relevant requirements interview work:
   331→- https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05828
   332→

   334→
   335→# 6. N3 — Ambiguity branching with behavioral divergence
   336→
   337→This is one of the most promising technical directions.
   338→
   339→Suppose the stakeholder says:
   340→
   341→> "The manager can approve requests quickly."
   342→
   343→The system identifies possible interpretations:
   344→
   345→```text
   346→Interpretation A:
   347→Manager can approve directly.
   348→
   349→Interpretation B:
   350→Manager approval is conditional on department.
   351→
   352→Interpretation C:
   353→Manager approval means two-step approval.
   354→```
   355→
   356→Instead of asking a generic question, the system generates a small **interpretation branch set**.
   357→
   358→For each branch, it constructs the expected downstream behavior:
   359→
   360→```text
   361→A → one approval state
   362→B → approval + department policy
   363→C → approval + second reviewer
   364→```
   365→
   366→Then it asks:
   367→
   368→> "Do you mean A, B, or C?"
   369→
   370→Or it asks a more natural question that separates the branches.
   371→
   372→This direction is related to ClarifyGPT and recent ambiguity-aware software engineering research, but the project can make the contribution specifically about **requirements-model branching and downstream behavioral divergence before implementation**.
   373→
   374→Related sources:
   375→- ClarifyGPT: https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.10996
   376→- Orchid ambiguous-requirement benchmark: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.21505
   377→

   380→# 7. N4 — A Requirement Uncertainty Ledger
   381→
   382→Create a first-class object called a **Requirement Uncertainty Ledger**.
   383→
   384→Every requirement may carry:
   385→
   386→```text
   387→certainty_state:
   388→    confirmed
   389→    likely
   390→    inferred
   391→    ambiguous
   392→    conflicting
   393→    blocked
   394→    obsolete
   395→```
   396→
   397→And:
   398→
   399→```text
   400→uncertainty_reasons:
   401→    missing_actor
   402→    missing_condition
   403→    vague_quantity
   404→    undefined_term
   405→    conflicting_stakeholders
   406→    missing_exception
   407→    missing_nfr
   408→    unsupported_inference
   409→```
   410→
   411→The product would show something like:
   412→
   413→```text
   414→Project Health
   415→--------------------------
   416→Confirmed:        31
   417→Likely:            8
   418→Inferred:         12
   419→Ambiguous:         5
   420→Conflicting:       2
   421→Blocked:           3
   422→
   423→High-risk uncertainty: 4
   424→```
   425→
   426→This converts "the project still has unclear requirements" into a measurable engineering artifact.
   427→
   428→Requirements uncertainty is a well-established topic, but a focused **LLM-era uncertainty ledger connected to evidence, downstream risk, and active questioning** was not identified in the specific LLM requirements sources reviewed for this project.
   429→
   430→Background:
   431→- https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666307421000218

   435→# 8. N5 — Requirement Debt as a continuously calculated metric
   436→
   437→Requirements Engineering Debt is an existing concept. The novelty should therefore not be "requirements debt exists."
   438→
   439→Instead, define an operational **Requirement Debt Score** over the project's live model.
   440→
   441→Example:
   442→
   443→```text
   444→Debt(REQ) =
   445→    ambiguity_weight
   446→  + missing_evidence_weight
   447→  + unresolved_conflict_weight
   448→  + untested_weight
   449→  + stale_dependency_weight
   450→  + unapproved_inference_weight
   451→```
   452→
   453→Then calculate:
   454→
   455→```text
   456→Project Requirement Debt
   457→├── Ambiguity debt
   458→├── Evidence debt
   459→├── Conflict debt
   460→├── Traceability debt
   461→├── Validation debt
   462→└── Staleness debt
   463→```
   464→
   465→The dashboard can show:
   466→
   467→```text
   468→REQ-021
   469→Requirement Debt: HIGH
   470→Reason:
   471→- no explicit source confirmation
   472→- references undefined "urgent"
   473→- affects 4 downstream tests
   474→- conflicts with REQ-033
   475→```
   476→
   477→Background:
   478→- https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.10887
   479→- https://doi.org/10.1016/j.infsof.2023.107201
   480→- 2025 exploration using LLMs/IDP for requirements debt: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1877050925025657

   484→# 9. N6 — Stakeholder Voice Preservation
   485→
   486→This is a strong product-level differentiator.
   487→
   488→Most requirement pipelines collapse all stakeholder statements into one document. The system should preserve **who wanted what, why, and whether the final requirement reflects or modifies that position**.
   489→
   490→Example:
   491→
   492→```text
   493→Stakeholder A — Customer Support
   494→Wants: cancellation allowed after assignment
   495→Reason: reduce complaints
   496→
   497→Stakeholder B — Operations
   498→Wants: cancellation blocked after dispatch
   499→Reason: delivery cost
   500→
   501→Stakeholder C — Finance
   502→Wants: refund only when payment is captured
   503→Reason: reconciliation constraints
   504→```
   505→
   506→The system creates:
   507→
   508→```text
   509→CONFLICT-004
   510→A ↔ B
   511→Topic: cancellation timing
   512→Status: unresolved
   513→Impact: refund workflow + dispatch workflow
   514→```
   515→
   516→After negotiation:
   517→
   518→```text
   519→Decision:
   520→Cancellation allowed until driver departure.
   521→Finance rule remains unchanged.
   522→```
   523→
   524→The final requirement links to **all original positions and the decision rationale**.
   525→
   526→This is different from merely detecting conflicts because it maintains a **stakeholder-voice ledger** through the life of the requirement.
   527→
   528→Caveat: stakeholder conflict resolution is a long-established RE topic, and multi-agent negotiation has now appeared in 2026. The proposed contribution is specifically the **provenance-preserving consolidation and lifecycle history**, not conflict detection itself.

   650→# 13. N10 — "Explain why you asked me this question"
   651→
   652→Every AI interview question can have hidden structured rationale:
   653→
   654→```text
   655→Question:
   656→"Can a manager approve a request after it expires?"
   657→
   658→Reason:
   659→- detected missing expiration behavior
   660→- affects REQ-012 and REQ-014
   661→- currently three valid interpretations exist
   662→- expected downstream impact = high
   663→- high expected information gain
   664→```
   665→
   666→The stakeholder sees only a concise explanation when needed, while the system stores the full reasoning metadata.
   667→
   668→This is valuable for trust and academic evaluation:
   669→
   670→> Can transparent question rationales increase stakeholder acceptance of AI-driven elicitation?

  1042→# 22. Evidence levels
  1043→
  1044→Use an explicit controlled vocabulary:
  1045→
  1046→```text
  1047→EXPLICIT
  1048→    Directly stated by a stakeholder.
  1049→
  1050→PARAPHRASED
  1051→    Restatement with no semantic change.
  1052→
  1053→DERIVED
  1054→    Logically derived from explicit requirements.
  1055→
  1056→LATENT
  1057→    Plausible opportunity inferred from workflow/context.
  1058→
  1059→ASSUMED
  1060→    Introduced to close a gap temporarily.
  1061→
  1062→MODEL_PROPOSED
  1063→    Proposed by the AI or analyst without stakeholder evidence.
  1064→
  1065→VALIDATED
  1066→    Explicitly approved by an authorized stakeholder.
  1067→
  1068→REJECTED
  1069→    Explicitly rejected.
  1070→```
  1071→
  1072→Never use `validated` as a synonym for `generated`.

  1120→# 24. Question utility formula
  1121→
  1122→Start with a transparent heuristic.
  1123→
  1124→```text
  1125→QScore(q) =
  1126→    α * InformationGain(q)
  1127→  + β * ImpactReduction(q)
  1128→  + γ * AmbiguityReduction(q)
  1129→  + δ * ConflictResolutionValue(q)
  1130→  + ε * CoverageGain(q)
  1131→  - ζ * UserEffort(q)
  1132→  - η * Redundancy(q)
  1133→```
  1134→
  1135→Then normalize every component to `[0,1]`.
  1136→
  1137→Suggested initial weights for an experiment:
  1138→
  1139→```text
  1140→α = 0.25
  1141→β = 0.25
  1142→γ = 0.20
  1143→δ = 0.15
  1144→ε = 0.10
  1145→ζ = 0.03
  1146→η = 0.02
  1147→```
  1148→
  1149→Do not treat these values as ground truth. They are starting points for an ablation study.
  1150→
  1151→Experiments should compare:
  1152→
  1153→```text
  1154→Baseline A: LLM chooses question
  1155→Baseline B: random eligible question
  1156→Baseline C: ontology-guided question selection
  1157→Proposed: impact-aware information-gain selection
  1158→```

  1162→# 25. Why downstream impact should affect the next question
  1163→
  1164→Example:
  1165→
  1166→```text
  1167→Unknown U1:

  1169→
  1170→Unknown U2:

  1250→# 27. Conflict detection
  1251→
  1252→Do not rely only on semantic similarity.
  1253→
  1254→Use a three-level approach:
  1255→
  1256→### Level 1 — lexical/semantic screening
  1257→
  1258→Retrieve candidate requirement pairs using embeddings.
  1259→
  1260→### Level 2 — LLM relation classifier
  1261→
  1262→Classify:
  1263→
  1264→```text
  1265→ENTAILS
  1266→CONTRADICTS
  1267→DEPENDS_ON
  1268→REFINES
  1269→OVERLAPS
  1270→UNRELATED
  1271→```
  1272→
  1273→### Level 3 — rule-aware validation
  1274→
  1275→Use deterministic checks for obvious domain constraints.
  1276→
  1277→Example:
  1278→
  1279→```text
  1280→REQ-A:
  1281→User can cancel after dispatch.
  1282→
  1283→REQ-B:
  1284→Cancellation is forbidden after dispatch.
  1285→```
  1286→
  1287→This should produce a conflict with evidence from both requirements.
  1288→
  1289→For Arabic conflict detection, AraREQ is a useful external benchmark and baseline.

  1472→# 32. Requirement debt engine
  1473→
  1474→Calculate a score at requirement level.
  1475→
  1476→Suggested formula:
  1477→
  1478→```text
  1479→DebtScore =
  1480→  20 * ambiguity
  1481→+ 15 * missing_evidence
  1482→+ 15 * unresolved_conflict
  1483→+ 10 * missing_acceptance_criteria
  1484→+ 10 * missing_tests
  1485→+ 10 * stale_traceability
  1486→+ 10 * unresolved_assumptions
  1487→+ 10 * age_without_review
  1488→```
  1489→
  1490→Normalize to `0–100`.
  1491→
  1492→Interpretation:
  1493→
  1494→```text
  1495→0–19    Low
  1496→20–39   Moderate
  1497→40–59   Significant
  1498→60–79   High
  1499→80–100  Critical
  1500→```
  1501→
  1502→These thresholds are engineering choices for the prototype, not scientifically established constants. Validate or calibrate them with users.

  1506→# 33. Human-in-the-loop approval policy
  1507→

  1600→
  1601→The LLM should not directly output one final question.
  1602→
  1603→First produce candidates:
  1604→
  1605→```json

  1626→# 37. Why deterministic post-processing matters
  1627→
  1628→Many recent requirements papers show a recurring pattern:
  1629→
  1630→```text
  1631→LLM generation
  1632→      ↓
  1633→structured post-processing
  1634→      ↓
  1635→validation
  1636→```
  1637→
  1638→ProReFiCIA, for example, combines prompting with refinement and filtering rather than trusting one raw output. Formal-requirements research also emphasizes coupling LLM flexibility with deterministic/formal validation.
  1639→
  1640→Relevant source:
  1641→- https://doi.org/10.1016/j.infsof.2025.107697
  1642→
  1643→The project should follow this design principle:
  1644→
  1645→> **LLM proposes; deterministic logic verifies wherever possible.**
  1646→

  1649→# 38. Formal validation rules
  1650→
  1651→Examples:
  1652→
  1653→```text
  1654→Rule R1:
  1655→Every validated requirement must have >= 1 evidence source or an explicitly accepted assumption.
  1656→
  1657→Rule R2:
  1658→Every acceptance criterion must reference a requirement.
  1659→
  1660→Rule R3:
  1661→Every high-priority functional requirement must have >= 1 acceptance criterion.
  1662→
  1663→Rule R4:
  1664→Every open conflict must have a status.
  1665→
  1666→Rule R5:
  1667→A latent requirement cannot become validated without human approval.
  1668→
  1669→Rule R6:
  1670→A changed requirement must invalidate or re-check affected downstream artifacts.
  1671→
  1672→Rule R7:
  1673→A superseded requirement remains queryable historically.
  1674→
  1675→Rule R8:
  1676→A requirement cannot be deleted if downstream artifacts depend on it; it must be superseded or archived.
  1677→```
