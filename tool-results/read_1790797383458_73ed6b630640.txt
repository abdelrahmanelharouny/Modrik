     1→     1→# RequirementPilot
     2→     2→## Agentic, Evidence-Grounded Requirements Engineering for Real Stakeholder Conversations
     3→     3→
     4→     4→> **Project reference document — research + product + implementation blueprint**
     5→     5→>
     6→     6→> **Research snapshot:** 30 September 2026
     7→     7→>
     8→     8→> **Status:** Graduation / Internship Project Reference
     9→     9→>
    10→    10→> **Working title:** RequirementPilot
    11→    11→>
    12→    12→> **Core idea:** Build an AI requirements engineer that does not merely generate requirements from a prompt. It conducts a structured stakeholder interview, identifies missing/ambiguous/conflicting information, preserves evidence and decisions, turns validated information into a living requirements model, and propagates approved changes through downstream artifacts.
    13→    13→
    14→    14→---
    15→    15→
    16→    16→## 0. Important positioning decision
    17→    17→
    18→    18→The original idea of a generic **"AI Requirements Engineer"** is no longer sufficiently novel by itself. Research and commercial systems already cover requirement extraction, user-story generation, acceptance-criteria generation, conversational elicitation, latent requirement discovery, conflict detection, change-impact analysis, requirements-to-code traceability, and multi-agent requirements/architecture workflows.
    19→    19→
    20→    20→Therefore, this project must **not** be presented as:
    21→    21→
    22→    22→> "An LLM that converts stakeholder text into requirements."
    23→    23→
    24→    24→The project should instead be positioned as:
    25→    25→
    26→    26→> **"A human-in-the-loop, evidence-grounded, change-aware requirements engineering system that actively interviews stakeholders, manages uncertainty and conflicts, and maintains a traceable requirements lifecycle."**
    27→    27→
    28→    28→The strongest differentiating direction is not one isolated feature. It is the **integration of several RE controls into one living, auditable system**, plus a focused research contribution around **question selection, evidence/provenance, uncertainty, and Arabic/Egyptian-Arabic mixed-language requirements elicitation**.
    29→    29→
    30→    30→**Novelty warning:** this document deliberately does **not** claim that every proposed feature is globally unprecedented. The literature is changing extremely quickly. The novelty statements below mean that, in the sources searched for this project, an **exact combination or exact implementation was not found**. Before submitting a paper or claiming "first," perform a final systematic search and citation check.

    34→    34→# 1. Executive Summary
    35→    35→
    36→    36→## 1.1 The problem
    37→    37→
    38→    38→Real stakeholders rarely provide clean software requirements.
    39→    39→
    40→    40→A stakeholder may say:
    41→    41→
    42→    42→> "عايز العميل يقدر يلغي الأوردر، بس بعد ما السواق يتحرك الموضوع يختلف، ولو المشكلة من عندنا الفلوس لازم ترجعله. وكمان المدير يشوف كل حاجة."
    43→    43→
    44→    44→This single statement contains multiple requirement candidates, exceptions, an undefined policy boundary, an actor distinction, and an implied business rule.
    45→    45→
    46→    46→A conventional LLM can immediately generate polished user stories. That is dangerous because the polished language can hide missing information rather than solve it.
    47→    47→
    48→    48→The real requirements-engineering problem is:
    49→    49→
    50→    50→1. identify what was actually stated;
    51→    51→2. distinguish explicit needs from inferred possibilities;
    52→    52→3. detect missing information;
    53→    53→4. detect ambiguous language;
    54→    54→5. detect conflicts between requirements or stakeholders;
    55→    55→6. ask the next question that provides the most useful clarification;
    56→    56→7. preserve source evidence and rationale;
    57→    57→8. transform validated answers into structured requirements;
    58→    58→9. connect requirements to acceptance criteria, tests, design artifacts and implementation tasks;
    59→    59→10. detect downstream impact when a requirement changes;
    60→    60→11. maintain a history of what changed, why, who approved it, and what remains unresolved.
    61→    61→

    64→    64→# 2. What existing research already covers
    65→    65→
    66→    66→The project was benchmarked against recent research through September 2026.
    67→    67→
    68→    68→## 2.1 RECOVER — requirements from stakeholder conversations
    69→    69→
    70→    70→**RECOVER (Requirements EliCitation frOm conVERsations)** analyzes stakeholder conversations and automatically extracts system requirements. It evaluates both turn-level identification and conversation-level synthesis, including correctness, completeness and actionability. The work appeared in *IEEE Transactions on Software Engineering* and was also presented at ICSE 2026.
    71→    71→
    72→    72→Key implication for this project:
    73→    73→
    74→    74→> "I analyze stakeholder conversations and generate requirements" is already known.
    75→    75→
    76→    76→Source:
    77→    77→- IEEE / institutional record: https://doi.org/10.1109/TSE.2025.3572056
    78→    78→- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19552
    79→    79→- ICSE 2026: https://conf.researchr.org/details/icse-2026/icse-2026-journal-first-papers/71/RECOVER-Toward-Requirements-Generation-from-Stakeholders-Conversations
    80→    80→
    81→    81→---
    82→    82→
    83→    83→## 2.2 LENS — latent requirements from stakeholder interviews
    84→    84→
    85→    85→A June 2026 preprint, **LENS (LLM-Enabled Needs Discovery from Stakeholder Interviews)**, goes beyond explicit extraction. It analyzes interview transcripts and organizational context to infer **latent requirements** that stakeholders did not directly articulate. Each inferred requirement is accompanied by rationale and transcript traceability. The preliminary industrial evaluation used twelve cybersecurity-operations interviews and reported an average explicit-requirement F1 of 84.4%; 75% of latent requirements were rated by domain experts as having useful automation/time-saving potential.
    86→    86→
    87→    87→Key implication:
    88→    88→
    89→    89→> "We will discover hidden/implicit requirements from conversations" is no longer safe as the only novelty.
    90→    90→
    91→    91→Source:
    92→    92→- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25867
    93→    93→
    94→    94→Important detail: LENS is especially relevant because it already uses an **explicit vs. latent requirement distinction**, organizational context, rationale generation, and source traceability.
    95→    95→
    96→    96→---
    97→    97→
    98→    98→## 2.3 OntoAgent — agentic requirements interview
    99→    99→
   100→   100→A May 2026 paper, **From Chat to Interview: Agentic Requirements Elicitation with an Experience Ontology**, introduces OntoAgent. It argues that free-form LLM chat can miss implicit requirements and ask redundant questions. OntoAgent first induces an **experience ontology** from domain requirements and then uses operations called `ParseUser`, `ScoreOnto`, `ReRankOnto`, and `GatePrune` to select requirement concerns and generate interview questions.
   101→   101→
   102→   102→The paper evaluates the approach on website-application requirements and reports a 33% improvement in IRE and 21% improvement in TKQR relative to the comparison baselines.
   103→   103→
   104→   104→Key implication:
   105→   105→
   106→   106→> "Our novelty is an agent that interviews the stakeholder instead of just chatting" is already being actively researched.
   107→   107→
   108→   108→Source:
   109→   109→- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05828
   110→   110→
   111→   111→Related code/evaluation ecosystem:
   112→   112→- ReqElicitBench / ReqElicitGym: https://github.com/jdm4pku/ReqElicitBench
   113→   113→
   114→   114→---

   116→   116→## 2.4 ReqElicitGym — evaluation of interview competence
   117→   117→
   118→   118→The ReqElicitGym repository provides an interactive evaluation environment for conversational requirements elicitation. It contains 101 website requirements-elicitation scenarios across 10 application types, an LLM-based simulated stakeholder, a judge, conversation logs, and quantitative metrics.
   119→   119→
   120→   120→Key implication:
   121→   121→
   122→   122→> The project should not invent an evaluation strategy from scratch; it should build on established benchmark ideas while adding a new test dimension such as Arabic/Egyptian Arabic, evidence quality, or cross-artifact impact.
   123→   123→

   129→   129→## 2.5 ProReFiCIA — requirements change-impact analysis
   130→   130→
   131→   131→ProReFiCIA is an LLM-driven change-impact analysis approach published in 2026. It takes a natural-language change rationale and identifies the affected requirements. The final version reports 85.7% recall on an unseen industrial dataset while requiring review of only about 3.0% of requirements; adding domain knowledge through RAG raised recall to 95.8% while increasing the review share to about 3.4%.
   132→   132→
   133→   133→Its workflow includes prompt optimization, model/prompt selection, refinement, filtering, and domain-knowledge augmentation.
   134→   134→
   135→   135→Key implication:
   136→   136→
   137→   137→> "Our system tells which requirements are affected when one requirement changes" is also already a standalone research contribution.
   138→   138→
   139→   139→Source:
   140→   140→- arXiv HTML: https://arxiv.org/html/2511.00262v6
   141→   141→- arXiv record: https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.00262

   145→   145→## 2.6 Arabic requirements conflict detection — AraREQ
   146→   146→
   147→   147→AraREQ, published at LREC 2026, provides a large Arabic dataset for requirements conflict detection and resolution.    149→   149→Key implication:
   150→   150→
   151→   151→> "We will make requirements conflict detection in Arabic" is now a documented research line.

   159→   159→## 2.7 Arabic ambiguity detection is not new either

   161→   161→Arabic requirements elicitation and ambiguity have been studied well before the current LLM wave.    168→   168→Key implication:
   169→   169→
   170→   170→The project should not claim simply:
   171→   171→
   172→   172→> "Arabic requirements are under-researched."
   173→   173→
   174→   174→A more defensible claim is:
   175→   175→
   176→   176→> "Modern LLM-based, evidence-grounded, multi-turn requirements engineering for Egyptian Arabic / code-switched Arabic-English stakeholder conversations remains a focused opportunity, especially when evaluated end-to-end rather than on isolated classification tasks."

   201→   201→## 2.9 Multi-agent architecture from requirements is already active
   202→   202→
   203→   203→MAAD (Multi-Agent Architecture Design), introduced in 2026, uses four agents — Analyst, Modeler, Designer and Evaluator — plus RAG and hierarchical memory to transform requirements into architecture blueprints. It evaluates architecture completeness, modularity, traceability and quality attributes.
   204→   204→
   205→   205→Sources:
   206→   206→- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01385
   207→   207→- replication package: https://github.com/RuiyinL/MAAD
   208→   208→
   209→   209→Therefore, the project should not claim novelty merely by adding multiple agents.
   210→   210→

   212→   212→
   213→   213→## 2.10 Multi-agent stakeholder negotiation is also emerging
   214→   214→
   215→   215→MedNegotiator, published in 2026, applies persona-driven generative AI agents, utility functions, Nash bargaining and safety guards to requirements negotiation in healthcare.
   216→   216→
   217→   217→Source:
   218→   218→- https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2590005626001062
   219→   219→
   220→   220→This means that "multiple agents negotiate requirements" is not untouched territory. A differentiated implementation would need a different research question or context.

   224→   224→# 3. What the literature suggests is still worth building
   225→   225→
   226→   226→After comparing these lines of work, the project should aim at **integration + measurable engineering controls**, not another isolated generator.
   227→   227→
   228→   228→The strongest project identity is:
   229→   229→
   230→   230→> **RequirementPilot is an evidence-first, change-aware, human-in-the-loop requirements operating system for ambiguous stakeholder conversations.**
   231→   231→
   232→   232→The system treats a requirement as a living engineering object, not as a sentence generated by an LLM.

   236→   236→# 4. Proposed novelty contributions
   237→   237→
   238→   238→The following are **candidate novelty contributions**, ordered from the most central to optional.
   239→   239→
   240→   240→## N1 — Evidence-first Requirement Objects
   241→   241→
   242→   242→Every requirement in the system must contain provenance and evidence metadata.
   243→   243→
   244→   244→A requirement should never exist as only:
   245→   245→
   246→   246→```text
   247→   247→REQ-017: The customer can cancel an order before dispatch.
   248→   248→```
   249→   249→
   250→   250→Instead, it should be modeled as:
   251→   251→
   252→   252→```json
   253→   253→{
   254→   254→  "id": "REQ-017",
   255→   255→  "statement": "The customer can cancel an order before dispatch.",
   256→   256→  "type": "functional",
   257→   257→  "status": "proposed",
   258→   258→  "source_spans": ["conversation_12", "turn_38"],
   259→   259→  "evidence_level": "explicit",
   260→   260→  "evidence_text": "...",
   261→   261→  "speaker": "customer",
   262→   262→  "stakeholder_role": "customer",
   263→   263→  "rationale": "...",
   264→   264→  "assumptions": [],
   265→   265→  "ambiguities": [],
   266→   266→  "confidence": 0.96,
   267→   267→  "validated_by": null,
   268→   268→  "approval_status": "pending",
   269→   269→  "created_by": "elicitation_agent",
   270→   270→  "created_at": "...",
   271→   271→  "version": 1
   272→   272→}
   273→   273→```
   274→   274→
   275→   275→### Why this matters
   276→   276→
   277→   277→It creates a distinction between:
   278→   278→
   279→   279→- what the stakeholder explicitly said;
   280→   280→- what the system inferred;
   281→   281→- what the analyst proposed;
   282→   282→- what a stakeholder validated;
   283→   283→- what remains uncertain.
   284→   284→
   285→   285→The system must never silently convert an inference into an official requirement.
   286→   286→
   287→   287→### Candidate research question
   288→   288→
   289→   289→> Does explicit evidence classification and provenance reduce hallucinated or unsupported requirements compared with conventional LLM-only requirements generation?
   290→   290→
   291→   291→---
   292→   292→
   293→   293→# 5. N2 — Adaptive Question Selection using uncertainty + information gain + impact
   294→   294→
   295→   295→OntoAgent already uses ontology-guided question selection. The project can go in a different direction by treating the interview as an **active uncertainty-reduction problem**.
   296→   296→
   297→   297→At each turn, the system maintains a set of unresolved requirement uncertainties:
   298→   298→
   299→   299→```text
   300→   300→U1: Who can cancel an order?
   301→   301→U2: When can cancellation occur?
   302→   302→U3: Is refund automatic?
   303→   303→U4: What exceptions exist?
   304→   304→U5: What happens if payment already settled?
   305→   305→```
   306→   306→
   307→   307→Each candidate question gets a utility score:
   308→   308→
   309→   309→```text
   310→   310→Question Utility =
   311→   311→    Information Gain
   312→   312→  × Requirement Impact
   313→   313→  × Ambiguity Reduction
   314→   314→  × Downstream Risk
   315→   315→  - User Effort
   316→   316→  - Redundancy
   317→   317→```
   318→   318→
   319→   319→The question is not simply "What sounds reasonable?"
   320→   320→
   321→   321→It is:
   322→   322→
   323→   323→> "Which unanswered question will remove the most uncertainty from the requirements model while causing the least stakeholder burden?"
   324→   324→
   325→   325→Expected information gain is an established concept for asking informative questions in LLM systems, so the novelty here should be the **requirements-engineering objective function and downstream impact weighting**, not the information-gain concept itself.
   326→   326→
   327→   327→Relevant general research:
   328→   328→- https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.291/
   329→   329→
   330→   330→Relevant requirements interview work:
   331→   331→- https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05828
   332→   332→
   333→   333→---
   334→   334→
   335→   335→# 6. N3 — Ambiguity branching with behavioral divergence
   336→   336→
   337→   337→This is one of the most promising technical directions.
   338→   338→
   339→   339→Suppose the stakeholder says:
   340→   340→
   341→   341→> "The manager can approve requests quickly."
   342→   342→
   343→   343→The system identifies possible interpretations:
   344→   344→
   345→   345→```text
   346→   346→Interpretation A:
   347→   347→Manager can approve directly.
   348→   348→
   349→   349→Interpretation B:
   350→   350→Manager approval is conditional on department.
   351→   351→
   352→   352→Interpretation C:
   353→   353→Manager approval means two-step approval.
   354→   354→```
   355→   355→
   356→   356→Instead of asking a generic question, the system generates a small **interpretation branch set**.
   357→   357→
   358→   358→For each branch, it constructs the expected downstream behavior:
   359→   359→
   360→   360→```text
   361→   361→A → one approval state
   362→   362→B → approval + department policy
   363→   363→C → approval + second reviewer
   364→   364→```
   365→   365→
   366→   366→Then it asks:
   367→   367→
   368→   368→> "Do you mean A, B, or C?"
   369→   369→
   370→   370→Or it asks a more natural question that separates the branches.
   371→   371→
   372→   372→This direction is related to ClarifyGPT and recent ambiguity-aware software engineering research, but the project can make the contribution specifically about **requirements-model branching and downstream behavioral divergence before implementation**.
   373→   373→
   374→   374→Related sources:
   375→   375→- ClarifyGPT: https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.10996
   376→   376→- Orchid ambiguous-requirement benchmark: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.21505
   377→   377→

   380→   380→# 7. N4 — A Requirement Uncertainty Ledger
   381→   381→
   382→   382→Create a first-class object called a **Requirement Uncertainty Ledger**.
   383→   383→
   384→   384→Every requirement may carry:
   385→   385→
   386→   386→```text
   387→   387→certainty_state:
   388→   388→    confirmed
   389→   389→    likely
   390→   390→    inferred
   391→   391→    ambiguous
   392→   392→    conflicting
   393→   393→    blocked
   394→   394→    obsolete
   395→   395→```
   396→   396→
   397→   397→And:
   398→   398→
   399→   399→```text
   400→   400→uncertainty_reasons:
   401→   401→    missing_actor
   402→   402→    missing_condition
   403→   403→    vague_quantity
   404→   404→    undefined_term
   405→   405→    conflicting_stakeholders
   406→   406→    missing_exception
   407→   407→    missing_nfr
   408→   408→    unsupported_inference
   409→   409→```
   410→   410→
   411→   411→The product would show something like:
   412→   412→
   413→   413→```text
   414→   414→Project Health
   415→   415→--------------------------
   416→   416→Confirmed:        31
   417→   417→Likely:            8
   418→   418→Inferred:         12
   419→   419→Ambiguous:         5
   420→   420→Conflicting:       2
   421→   421→Blocked:           3
   422→   422→
   423→   423→High-risk uncertainty: 4
   424→   424→```
   425→   425→
   426→   426→This converts "the project still has unclear requirements" into a measurable engineering artifact.
   427→   427→
   428→   428→Requirements uncertainty is a well-established topic, but a focused **LLM-era uncertainty ledger connected to evidence, downstream risk, and active questioning** was not identified in the specific LLM requirements sources reviewed for this project.
   429→   429→
   430→   430→Background:
   431→   431→- https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666307421000218

   435→   435→# 8. N5 — Requirement Debt as a continuously calculated metric
   436→   436→
   437→   437→Requirements Engineering Debt is an existing concept. The novelty should therefore not be "requirements debt exists."
   438→   438→
   439→   439→Instead, define an operational **Requirement Debt Score** over the project's live model.
   440→   440→
   441→   441→Example:
   442→   442→
   443→   443→```text
   444→   444→Debt(REQ) =
   445→   445→    ambiguity_weight
   446→   446→  + missing_evidence_weight
   447→   447→  + unresolved_conflict_weight
   448→   448→  + untested_weight
   449→   449→  + stale_dependency_weight
   450→   450→  + unapproved_inference_weight
   451→   451→```
   452→   452→
   453→   453→Then calculate:
   454→   454→
   455→   455→```text
   456→   456→Project Requirement Debt
   457→   457→├── Ambiguity debt
   458→   458→├── Evidence debt
   459→   459→├── Conflict debt
   460→   460→├── Traceability debt
   461→   461→├── Validation debt
   462→   462→└── Staleness debt
   463→   463→```
   464→   464→
   465→   465→The dashboard can show:
   466→   466→
   467→   467→```text

   484→   484→# 9. N6 — Stakeholder Voice Preservation
   485→   485→
   486→   486→This is a strong product-level differentiator.
   487→   487→
   488→   488→Most requirement pipelines collapse all stakeholder statements into one document. The system should preserve **who wanted what, why, and whether the final requirement reflects or modifies that position**.
   489→   489→
   490→   490→Example:
   491→   491→
   492→   492→```text
   493→   493→Stakeholder A — Customer Support
   494→   494→Wants: cancellation allowed after assignment
   495→   495→Reason: reduce complaints
   496→   496→
   497→   497→Stakeholder B — Operations
   498→   498→Wants: cancellation blocked after dispatch
   499→   499→Reason: delivery cost
   500→   500→
   501→   501→Stakeholder C — Finance
   502→   502→Wants: refund only when payment is captured
   503→   503→Reason: reconciliation constraints
   504→   504→```
   505→   505→
   506→   506→The system creates:
   507→   507→
   508→   508→```text
   509→   509→CONFLICT-004
   510→   510→A ↔ B
   511→   511→Topic: cancellation timing
   512→   512→Status: unresolved
   513→   513→Impact: refund workflow + dispatch workflow
   514→   514→```
   515→   515→
   516→   516→After negotiation:
   517→   517→
   518→   518→```text
   519→   519→Decision:
   520→   520→Cancellation allowed until driver departure.
   521→   521→Finance rule remains unchanged.
   522→   522→```
   523→   523→
   524→   524→The final requirement links to **all original positions and the decision rationale**.
   525→   525→
   526→   526→This is different from merely detecting conflicts because it maintains a **stakeholder-voice ledger** through the life of the requirement.
   527→   527→
   528→   528→Caveat: stakeholder conflict resolution is a long-established RE topic, and multi-agent negotiation has now appeared in 2026. The proposed contribution is specifically the **provenance-preserving consolidation and lifecycle history**, not conflict detection itself.

  1060→  1060→    Introduced to close a gap temporarily.

  1120→  1120→# 24. Question utility formula
  1121→  1121→
  1122→  1122→Start with a transparent heuristic.
  1123→  1123→

  1129→  1129→  + δ * ConflictResolutionValue(q)

  1250→  1250→# 27. Conflict detection
  1251→  1251→
  1252→  1252→Do not rely only on semantic similarity.
  1253→  1253→
  1254→  1254→Use a three-level approach:
  1255→  1255→
  1256→  1256→### Level 1 — lexical/semantic screening
  1257→  1257→
  1258→  1258→Retrieve candidate requirement pairs using embeddings.
  1259→  1259→
  1260→  1260→### Level 2 — LLM relation classifier
  1261→  1261→
  1262→  1262→Classify:
  1263→  1263→
  1264→  1264→```text
  1265→  1265→ENTAILS
  1266→  1266→CONTRADICTS
  1267→  1267→DEPENDS_ON
  1268→  1268→REFINES
  1269→  1269→OVERLAPS
  1270→  1270→UNRELATED
  1271→  1271→```
  1272→  1272→
  1273→  1273→### Level 3 — rule-aware validation
  1274→  1274→
  1275→  1275→Use deterministic checks for obvious domain constraints.
  1276→  1276→
  1277→  1277→Example:
  1278→  1278→
  1279→  1279→```text

  1286→  1286→
  1287→  1287→This should produce a conflict with evidence from both requirements.
  1288→  1288→
  1289→  1289→For Arabic conflict detection, AraREQ is a useful external benchmark and baseline.

  1630→  1630→```text

  1644→  1644→
